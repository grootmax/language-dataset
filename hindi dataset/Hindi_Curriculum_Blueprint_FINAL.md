Permanent blueprint for dataset generation

From अ to असली हिंदी —
the whole Hindi journey
Fifteen modules take a complete beginner from the first vowel to real,
connected Hindi. The script is short because Devanagari is short. The weight
sits where Hindi actually breaks English speakers: gender, the oblique case,
and the ने construction.

MODULE 1 · Sounds & Vowels · स्वर
15 items · 5 levels · 1 checkpoint · mock 20Q
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Ear before eye. The 11 vowels, each taught with its matra shown alongside so
the letter and its sign are never learned as two separate things — plus four
orientation items on the contrasts English does not mark.

  L1    अ  आ  इ
  L2    ई  उ  ऊ
  L3    ए  ऐ  ओ
  L4    औ  ऋ  ·  contrast: aspirated vs unaspirated (क/ख, प/फ)
  L5    contrast: retroflex vs dental (ट/त, ड/द, ण/न)
        contrast: ं vs ँ  (हंस swan / हँस laugh)
        contrast: व — neither English v nor w

  Practice = hear it, pick the letter
  Game     = odd sound out (audio)

TEACHING NOTE — these four contrasts are at the very front on purpose. They
fossilize faster than anything else in Hindi. Two weeks here changes how the
learner sounds a year later.

MODULE 2 · Consonants · व्यंजन

TEACHING NOTE — SCHWA, STATED PRECISELY
Devanagari writes an inherent "a" that is frequently not pronounced. The two
positions behave differently and must be taught differently:

  WORD-FINAL — near-categorical deletion in modern standard Hindi.
    कमल = kamal (not kamala) · नमक = namak · घर = ghar
    Teach this as a firm rule. The learner can rely on it.
    Named exceptions: retained after most conjuncts where it aids
    pronounceability (मित्र, राष्ट्र), and in careful/recited Sanskrit
    register (कृष्ण as krishna in devotional contexts, krishn in speech).

  MEDIAL — variable, depends on word structure and conventional
    pronunciation. नमस्ते = namaste (schwa after म dropped) but समझ = samajh.
    Do NOT present a single exceptionless rule here. Teach the
    high-frequency patterns through audio and repeated reading.

Every vocabulary item in the entire dataset carries a verified pronunciation
field, generated once and reviewed by a native speaker:

    "word": "नमस्ते",
    "read_as": "namaste",
    "pronunciation_notes": "The inherent vowel after म is not pronounced."

TEACHING NOTE — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE IN THIS SPEC

Every noun card carries gender as a permanent, visible field. Not a grammar
lesson in some later module — a property of the word itself, present on the
learn card, tested on the game card, exactly the way German is taught with
der/die/das.

The -ा masculine / -ी feminine pattern covers roughly 70%. The rest must be
learned as pairs and should be visually FLAGGED:
  किताब (f)  चीज़ (f)  बात (f)  रात (f)  चाय (f)
  पानी (m)   आदमी (m)  दही (m)   मोती (m)  घी (m)

Retrofitting gender later is the single most painful correction in Hindi
teaching. Module 6 collapses without it.

MODULE 5 · First Sentences · पहला वाक्य

  L5–7    (9)  pronouns मैं तू तुम आप यह वह ये वे हम
  L8–9    (6)  THE REGISTER SYSTEM — तू / तुम / आप, a full block
  L10–11  (6)  यह/वह near–far · ये/वे plural
  L12–14  (9)  question words क्या कौन कहाँ कब कैसे क्यों कितना कौन सा
  L15–17  (9)  adjective agreement -ा / -े / -ी
  L18–20  (9)  postpositions में पर से को तक का के लिए के पास के साथ
  L21–22  (6)  negation नहीं / न / मत
  L23     (3)  SOV word order
  L24     (3)  BASIC CONNECTORS — और, लेकिन, क्योंकि, इसलिए, फिर, तो

  Practice = complete the sentence

REVISION NOTE — connectors were funded by trimming postpositions from 12
items to 9, not by cutting negation. Module 6 drills postpositions
exhaustively; those three items were redundant. नहीं/न/मत keeps its 6.

TEACHING NOTE — postpositions appear here but the oblique is NOT yet
demanded. Learners meet घर में and किताब पर, which happen to look
unchanged. Module 6 then explains why लड़का becomes लड़के को. Introducing
the form before the rule makes the rule feel like an answer, not a burden.

TEACHING NOTE — तू is taught as a warning, not an option. आप is the safe
default. तू will get a learner into genuine trouble.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MICRO-DIALOGUES · from Module 5 onward
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Module 12 remains the main listening module, but it must not be the
learner's first exposure to connected speech. From Module 5 onward, the
THIRD ITEM of every fifth level is delivered as a 2–4 line dialogue using
only language already taught.

  A: आप कहाँ रहते हैं?
  B: मैं चेन्नई में रहता हूँ।
  A: क्या आपको यह शहर पसंद है?
  B: हाँ, बहुत पसंद है।

These are NOT additional items — they are a delivery format for existing
items. Level, item and card counts are unchanged. Approximately 60
micro-dialogues result across Modules 5–15.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MODULE 6 · The Oblique Case · तिरछा रूप   ★ THE GATEWAY
66 items · 22 levels · 4 checkpoints · mock 30Q
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Placed here on purpose. Most textbooks bury the oblique around chapter 12.
Putting it in month two is the single biggest sequencing decision in this
curriculum — until it clicks, the learner cannot produce one correct
sentence containing a postposition, which is nearly every real sentence.

REBUILT AROUND SIX ACTUAL NOUN CLASSES. The earlier "feminine — singular
unchanged, plural -ों" was not merely coarse, it was wrong: feminine -ी
nouns take -ियों, other feminines take -ों. Dataset generation needs the
classes named explicitly.

  L1      (3)  the concept — every noun has two forms

  L2–4    (9)  CLASS 1 · marked masculine -ा
                 लड़का → लड़के को → लड़कों को
  L5–6    (6)  CLASS 2 · unmarked masculine
                 घर → घर में → घरों में
  L7–8    (6)  CLASS 3 · masculine ending -ी
                 आदमी → आदमी को → आदमियों को
  L9–10   (6)  CLASS 4 · feminine ending -ी
                 लड़की → लड़की को → लड़कियों को
  L11–12  (6)  CLASS 5 · other feminine
                 किताब → किताब में → किताबों में
  L13–14  (6)  MIXED-CLASS DRILL — the actual skill is choosing the class,
                 not applying one in isolation

  L15–17  (9)  adjectives in oblique      बड़ा घर → बड़े घर में
  L18–20  (9)  CLASS 6 · pronouns with postpositions
                 मैं → मुझे / मुझसे / मेरे साथ
                 वह → उसे / उससे / उसके लिए
  L21–22  (6)  का / के / की possession

  Practice = transform to oblique
  Game     = spot the error

TEACHING NOTE — the mixed-class drill at L13–14 is load-bearing. Learners
who drill each class in isolation pass every level and still fail in
production, because real sentences don't announce which class is coming.

TEACHING NOTE — का/के/की agrees with the POSSESSED noun, not the possessor:
  राम का बेटा  ·  राम की बेटी  ·  राम के बेटे
This is gender agreement's first real workout and where Module 4's gender
discipline pays off.

MODULE 9 · The ने Construction · ने       ★ HIGHEST-VALUE MODULE
48 items · 16 levels · 3 checkpoints · mock 30Q
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Its own module because it is the highest-frequency error in intermediate
Hindi. In perfective transitive sentences the subject takes ने — and the
verb then agrees with the OBJECT, not the subject.

  L1      (3)  the concept — why ने exists
  L2–3    (6)  ने + masculine singular object     मैंने खाना खाया
  L4–6    (9)  ने + feminine object agreement     मैंने रोटी खाई
  L7–8    (6)  ने + plural object                 मैंने रोटियाँ खाईं
  L9–10   (6)  INTRANSITIVE = NO ने               मैं गया (not मैंने गया)
  L11–12  (6)  the exceptions — बोलना, लाना, भूलना take no ने
  L13–14  (6)  को-marked object → neutral verb    मैंने उसको देखा
  L15–16  (6)  ने in perfect tenses               मैंने खाया है / था

  Practice = transform to perfective
  Game     = spot the error

TEACHING NOTE — drilling volume here is deliberately heavy relative to
module size. Every item forces an agreement decision, never a recognition.

MODULE 12 · Conversation · बातचीत          LISTENING ONLY
120 items · 40 levels · 8 checkpoints · mock 40Q
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
120 dialogues in natural spoken Hindi — real contractions, real fillers, not
textbook Hindi. Purely receptive. The learner listens and comprehends; they
do not speak, and the app does not pretend to check speech.

  L1–6    (18) shops, tea stall, greetings — slow, 4–6 lines
  L7–12   (18) auto & taxi, directions, transport
  L13–18  (18) restaurant, ordering, food
  L19–24  (18) doctor, pharmacy, health
  L25–30  (18) office, phone calls, appointments
  L31–36  (18) home, family, social visits
  L37–40  (12) fast natural speech, 3+ speakers, Hinglish-mixed

  CARD ARC
  learn      play the dialogue · Hindi + English line by line, every line
             tappable to replay · normal and slow playback
  practice   comprehension MCQ
  game       AUDIO GAP-FILL — dialogue replays with one word muted, learner
             picks from four options
  summary    the 2–3 useful phrases from that dialogue, isolated, replayable

TEACHING NOTE — the gap-fill does the real work. Cheap to build (same audio,
one mask) and it converts a passive listen into an active test. Mask
deliberately: a postposition, a ने, a gender-marked verb ending. Without it,
40 levels of listen-and-answer becomes skimmable within a week.

OPTIONAL — an ungraded "repeat after it" shadow button. No verification, no
score. Costs nothing and the mouth benefits regardless. Never framed as a
test.

PRODUCTION NOTE — 120 dialogues is the most expensive line in this spec. If
capacity is tight, cut to 30 levels (90 dialogues). Do NOT cut grammar
modules to protect it.

THE SIX DECISIONS WORTH DEFENDING
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1  GENDER AS A VOCABULARY PROPERTY, NEVER A GRAMMAR CHAPTER
   Baked into every word card from Module 4 level 1.

   And organised by the six real noun classes, with a mixed-class drill.

   Highest-frequency intermediate error in the language.

   Irregular to 100. Seven per module, outside the level flow.
